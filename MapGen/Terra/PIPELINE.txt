TERRA — the terrain generator
=============================

Replaces the ck2rpg magma simulation (still reachable via --legacy-terrain).
Heights are normalised to roughly [0,1] with sea level fixed at TerraSeaLevel
(0.30) and converted to the project's integer elevation scale only on the way
out, so a value means the same thing on every seed and at every map size.


THREE RESOLUTIONS
-----------------
  base      Width/4    tectonics + the main erosion. Cheap enough for ~34 iterations.
  province  Width/2    drainage, rivers, lakes. The raster CK3 reads rivers.png from.
  full      Width      detail, channel carving, the exported heightmap.

Nothing wraps in X. CK3's map is a flat rectangle with hard edges and forced
ocean borders, so wrapping would only cost speed.


THE STAGES
----------
1. ContinentBuilder            base
   fBm through a two-stage domain warp gives the coastline its peninsulas and
   bays. The land threshold is *solved* for TargetLandFraction by quantile —
   not grown — so reaching a land target no longer means inflating elevation
   everywhere. Land rises asymptotically to TerraContinentRise; ocean falls
   asymptotically to TerraOceanDepth. Both branches meet exactly at sea level:
   any offset there is a cliff around every shoreline on the map.
   Then AddInteriorRelief adds broad hills, ramped in above the waterline
   rather than applied as a step (same reason).

2. PlateTectonics              base
   Voronoi plates with drift vectors, sampled through a domain warp so plate
   edges are organic rather than straight. Boundary distance is (|d2|-|d1|);
   convergence is the closing speed of the two plates along their shared
   normal. Uplift is a narrow exponential core (TerraRangeWidth, well under 1%
   of map width) plus a wider weak flank for foothills, modulated along the
   belt by ridged noise so ranges have saddles and passes.
   A hard cutoff at coreWidth*14 is load-bearing: the strength multiplier is
   constant per *pair* of plates, so it jumps wherever the second-nearest
   plate changes — a line through the middle of a plate. Without the cutoff
   the whole tessellation shows as flat-shaded polygons.

3. LandscapeEvolution          base, ~34 iterations
   dh = U - K·A^m·S^n, plus sediment routing and a thermal slope limit.
   Uplift is fed in as a RATE, not stamped in as a height. That is what keeps
   a range a strip: the belt is rebuilt where the boundary is while rivers cut
   into it from both sides, instead of relaxing into a dome.
   Deterministic and parallel. Droplet erosion is the usual choice and looks
   comparable, but droplets write to shared cells, so a parallel version is
   not reproducible from a seed — which this project's whole test loop is.

4. Upscale + AddDetail         full
   Catmull-Rom upsample (bilinear leaves first-derivative creases along every
   source cell, invisible in greyscale but obvious once CK3 lights it).
   Detail is fBm-dominant with only a minor ridged term: it is a SUBSTRATE for
   stage 5 to cut, not the final shape. Leaning on ridged multifractal here
   bakes in concentric ring-and-spoke patterns that read as swirls.

5. Refinement erosion + rivers province
   Real erosion iterations, not a closed-form approximation — a drainage
   network is emergent from routing and incising repeatedly, so one pass of
   anything cannot branch. Three settings decide whether it produces dendritic
   texture: no thermal pass (it fills channels in as fast as they are cut), a
   low area exponent (~0.30; branching is made by headwaters, and a high
   exponent puts all incision into the few biggest rivers), and no uplift.
   The result is carried to full resolution as a DELTA so existing detail
   survives.
   Rivers are then read off the same flow field the erosion used, so a river
   cannot cross a ridge. Courses are simplified (Douglas-Peucker), resampled
   along a Catmull-Rom spline and given a discharge-scaled meander offset.
   Lakes are filled depressions above a minimum size — mask only, they are
   deliberately NOT emitted as water.

6. Relax + CarveChannels       full
   Slope-limited relaxation (banded with saved seam rows, so the answer does
   not depend on thread scheduling), then the river channels are cut.

7. TerraScale                  full
   Piecewise-linear onto the integer scale, with the knee on a percentile of
   this map's own land so a fixed share lands above the mountain line. Monotone:
   it moves nothing, only renames the heights. ProvinceElevation is re-derived
   AFTER every edit, so provinces.png and heightmap.png agree by construction.


HEIGHT SCALE (Emit/MapDataWriter)
---------------------------------
Land is ranked onto VanillaLandCurve, measured off vanilla's own heightmap.
Reproduces vanilla's percentiles exactly: 36 / 57 / 86 / 143 / 191.

Water is NOT ranked — it is mapped from actual depth. Ranking reproduces
vanilla's histogram (85% of water pure black) while getting the spatial result
wrong, because a pixel's rank depends on coastline length, not distance
offshore. Ours is less broken up than vanilla's, so the same 15% shelf
allocation spread far out to sea and the sea floor showed through the water.
TerraShelfDepth / TerraShelfCurve control it instead.


THINGS THAT WILL BITE
---------------------
* Anything expressed per grid cell is resolution-dependent. Slopes (talus,
  deposition slope, erodibility) go through SlopeScaleFor(); pixel lengths go
  through Scaled(), authored against vanilla's 9216-wide province map.
* WORLD_EXTENTS_Y and WATERLEVEL must scale together — vanilla's own comment
  pins the ratio at 3/50 = 0.06, and breaking it moves the waterline off
  19/255, which every curve here is built around.
* Land fraction drifts a few percent: the threshold is solved before erosion.


COST (seed 1234, end to end)
----------------------------
  tiny     ~2 s        small    ~8 s
  full     ~40 s       vanilla  ~4 min   (refinement erosion is ~90 s of it;
                                          TerraRefineIterations = 0 disables)

Knobs live in Config/MapConfig.cs, categorised in pipeline order.
Preview renders (hillshade, height, moisture, rivers) go to out/ via
Io/TerraPreview.cs — judge erosion on the hillshade, never flat greyscale.
