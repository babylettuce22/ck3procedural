PROCEDURAL ARMOUR — WHAT IS KNOWN
=================================

Written 2026-08-27, against CK3 1.19.0.6. Everything here was either measured on this machine or
learned by watching the game reject something. Where a thing is unverified it says so.

The armour forge is built and validates clean. Code lives in Emit/ArtifactForge/ alongside the
weapon forge: ArmorCatalogue.cs finds vanilla garments, ArmorForgeStep.cs selects and emits.


1. WHAT IT DOES
---------------
84 looks per world = 7 cultures x 6 armour types x 2 genders. Per look it emits an entity, an
accessory, a variation and a 16x4 palette — text plus a few hundred bytes. No geometry.

Culture picks the silhouette, artifact type picks the material. That split is forced, not chosen:
vanilla's war garments are organised by culture and era with no material in any name, and vanilla
collapses all six armour types onto two visuals of its own (`armor` 26 uses, `plate` 4). So type
cannot drive shape. It drives the palette instead.

Culture matching is real rather than arbitrary: generated cultures carry a vanilla `clothing_gfx`,
which already decides their ordinary clothes, so war dress is matched to it. east_slavic gets
steppe, byzantine gets byzantine, mongol gets mongol.


2. THE MECHANISM, END TO END
----------------------------
Five files have to agree or nothing shows:

  common/genes/05_genes_special_accessories_clothes.txt   our template, spliced into a vanilla copy
  gfx/portraits/accessories/00_gen_armor.txt              accessory -> entity
  gfx/models/artifacts/gen_armor/00_gen_armor.asset       entity -> vanilla mesh + our variation
  gfx/portraits/accessory_variations/00_gen_armor_*.txt   variation -> palette + swatch
  gfx/portraits/portrait_modifiers/zz_gen_artifact_armor  when to wear it

Armour is the mirror image of a weapon. A weapon costs nothing per artifact — the engine substitutes
its entity through `game_entity_override = weapon` — but needs an animation hook to be drawn at all.
Armour has no such override (`game_entity_override` has exactly ONE value in the whole game and it
is `weapon`), so it is built AGOT-style as a portrait modifier plus an accessory per look. That
needs no animation hook, because a `usage = game` group runs on every portrait, but it costs
emitted text per look.


3. THINGS THAT COST A ROUND TO LEARN
------------------------------------
** The engine enforces gene-template membership. ck3-tiger does not. **
   A portrait modifier's `accessory` must be a member of the `template` it names. Tiger checks only
   that the accessory exists somewhere, so a wrong template passes validation silently and the game
   says, 84 times, in error.log:
       Can't find accessory 'gen_armor_..._plate_m' in gene template most_clothes
   The garment then falls back to the culture's ordinary war dress — which is the SAME ART — so the
   failure looks like "the recolour did nothing" rather than like an error. Check error.log.

** Redeclaring an accessory gene REPLACES it. It does not merge. **
   A separate file containing `special_genes = { accessory_genes = { clothes = { our_template } } }`
   wipes every vanilla template. Tiger catches this one loudly (`most_clothes not found in category
   clothes`, 46 of them); in game it would strip clothing from every character alive.
   AGOT suggests otherwise but does not contradict it: AGOT adds a whole new GENE in its own file,
   and merging happens at `accessory_genes` — one level above the templates inside a single gene.
   The only route is to copy vanilla's gene file and splice a template in. Read the INSTALLED file
   at generation time and it tracks whatever patch is on the machine.

** A palette column is a BLOCK of four. **
   The shader indexes at `channel * 4 + <the swatch's own colormask channel>`. Our swatches fire red
   alone, so mask channel g reads column g*4 — columns 0, 4, 8, 12. Writing four colours into
   columns 0-3 tints only the red channel; the rest stay white, and white is not a colour there, it
   is "no tint". Three quarters of the garment keeps its vanilla diffuse and every armour type looks
   identical. The weapon recolour already had this right; the armour one was written from an older
   copy of the method.

** An accessory's GENDER is read from its name prefix. **
   Not from the block it sits in, not from the entity it points at — from the first characters of
   its own name. Across all 630 entries in vanilla's clothes gene there is not one exception: every
   name in a `male` block starts with m_ or male_, every name in a `female` block with f_ or
   female_. Named gen_armor_..._f, with the gender as a SUFFIX, all 42 female accessories were read
   as male; a female portrait then looked in the female list, found nothing it considered female,
   and fell back. All 42 male worked throughout, which is what made it look like a female-only bug
   rather than a naming rule. Names are now f_gen_armor_... and m_gen_armor_....

** Every accessory template needs `boy = male` and `girl = female`. **
   197 of vanilla's 197 clothes templates carry both. A template without them does not fail to
   parse and ck3-tiger says nothing, but female lookups into it do not resolve. The two lines map
   children onto their sex's adult list, so it is not obvious why adult women depend on them — but
   they do, and it was the last structural difference between our template and vanilla's.

   Both of these were found the same way: count how vanilla does a thing across every instance, and
   treat 100%-with-no-exceptions as a rule rather than a habit. That heuristic has now caught two
   bugs that tiger passed and that the files themselves looked fine under.

** A pattern shader with no pattern data reads RUBBISH. It does not fall back. **
   The custom breastplate was emitted with the shader its mesh carried,
   portrait_attachment_pattern_alpha_to_coverage, but with no `portrait_accessory` block — so no
   pattern_mask and no variation were ever bound. portrait_accessory_variation.fxh opens with
       float4 Mask = PdxTex2D( PatternMask, Input.UV0 );
   and with nothing bound that samples whatever texture is left in the slot: in practice the
   clothing the character is already wearing, whose pattern textures are bound for its own draw.
   The piece came out patched with black in a pattern that CHANGED WITH THE OUTFIT, and looked
   clean on a bare-chested character because nothing else had bound pattern data. Two symptoms
   that looked unrelated; one cause.

   A piece with no variation must use the plain shader. The generator now strips `_pattern` from
   whatever the mesh was exported with — portrait_attachment (103 vanilla uses) and
   portrait_attachment_alpha_to_coverage (67) both exist, so alpha coverage survives the swap.

** There are TWO `_properties.dds` conventions and they are NOT the same. **
   Confusing them is a live risk: "correcting" our swatch to the model convention would break the
   working weapon recolour. Both were read out of the shader, not inferred.

     MODEL properties (a mesh's texture_specular), portrait.shader line 275:
         GetMaterialProperties( Diffuse, Normal, saturate(Properties.a), Properties.g, Properties.b )
       -> A = roughness, G = spec, B = metalness.
          R is NOT AO here - it is the scattering / second-colour mask (lines 293, 479), which is
          why vanilla's model maps have R black. Written as `- - M R`.

     PATTERN SWATCH properties (a pattern_textures entry), portrait_accessory_variation.fxh 171:
         Diffuse.rgb *= PatternProperties.rrr;   // the comment in the file says "pattern AO"
       -> R IS AO here, and defaults to 1.0 (line 134) where no pattern applies.
          Our gen_steel swatch is R=255 AO, G=0 spec, B=230 metal, A=51 rough. Correct as it is.

** Normal maps are RRxG. Blue is not normal-Z. **
   portrait.shader unpacks with UnpackRRxGNormal: X from R, Y from G, blue unused for the normal.
   So a bump photo dropped in whole does not give a tangent normal - the shader reads two channels
   that happen to contain photo luminance. (A claim that blue is EMISSIVE is UNVERIFIED here:
   `emissive` appears nowhere in portrait.shader. It may hold for another shader; it does not
   demonstrably hold for this one.)

** Armour visuals are evaluated per render. Weapon visuals are not. **
   An artifact's `visuals` entry resolves once, INSIDE create_artifact — proven from the call chain
   in error.log. A portrait modifier is different: it runs every time the character is drawn. So for
   armour, gating on the wearer's culture would repaint a stolen cuirass in the thief's colours.
   Gate on `creator ?= { ... }` instead; the creator never changes. Vanilla's own artifact triggers
   use exactly that form, with the owner as fallback.

** The coat of arms on the tabard is a shader. **
   42 war garments use `portrait_attachment_with_coa_and_variations`, which paints the wearer's
   dynasty on. `portrait_attachment_pattern` is the same shader without that stage (574 vanilla
   assets use it). BUT the swap only applies where the entity declares `meshsettings` — many do not,
   including all six a default world currently picks, and those take their shader from the .mesh
   material instead. Not a gap worth closing: the garments with no meshsettings are already on a
   pattern shader, because the CoA variants are the ones vanilla configures explicitly.

----------------------------------------
custom_breastplate_01 — from a Krakow museum scan, CC0, decimated 261856 -> 6002 tris.

  >> BLENDER_HANDOFF.md sitting next to this file. That document's facing convention is
  >> inverted, and every number in it that keys off "front-most = max z" is inverted with it.

Measured, and it is in good shape:
  verts 18006, tris 6002        (verts fully split, 3x tris - normal for exported split normals)
  skin present                  bones = 4 influences/vertex, ix 4/vert, w 4/vert
  skeleton                      134 bones
  UV2 present                   18006 uvs - so it can take a pattern swatch
  shader                        portrait_attachment_pattern_alpha_to_coverage (correct, no CoA)
  textures                      diffuse / normal / properties, all three present

** ONE BLOCKER, and it is a rename. **
   The bone NAMES do not match vanilla's. Same 134 bones, same order, identical suffixes — only the
   prefix and separator differ:

       vanilla   male_body_skeleton_01:ground_joint
       ours      male_body_rig_01_ground_joint

   Bones bind by name, so as exported this will not attach to the portrait body. The rig itself is
   correct; only the naming is wrong. Two ways to fix:
     a) rename the armature in Blender to `male_body_skeleton_01` and get the colon separator, or
     b) do it on our side — Io/PdxMesh.cs reads and writes these files, and rewriting 134 node names
        in the skeleton block touches nothing else.
   (b) is probably better, because it fixes every future export without the Blender side having to
   remember. Unverified either way: nothing has been through the engine yet.

** Also missing: `sfs` **
   Vanilla worn garments carry an `sfs` property (4 floats) on the mesh node. Ours does not. Meaning
   still unknown — it was flagged as unverified in the weapon handoff and still is. Find out before
   assuming it can be left off.


5B. WHICH WAY IS FRONT  (corrected 2026-08-27 - the earlier convention was backwards)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  THE CHARACTER FACES  -z  IN FILE SPACE  =  -Y IN BLENDER.

BLENDER_HANDOFF.md states the opposite ("z is front/back with +z toward the viewer") and every
measurement in it that took the front-most surface as MAX z is therefore measuring the BACK. That
includes its radius table, which was comparing the plate's back against the garment's back.

HOW IT WAS SETTLED - three independent checks, all agreeing, none relying on eyeballing a render:

  1. The body mesh. gfx/models/portraits/male_body/male_body.mesh viewed from -Y shows pectorals,
     navel, kneecaps and the tops of the feet with toes. That is a front.
  2. The rig. male_body_rig_01_bn_l_ftToe sits at -Y relative to bn_l_ftBall. Toes lead.
  3. The body's feet. Vertices in the lowest 6% of the figure span Y -16.81..+10.60 with median
     -9.75: the mass (forefoot and toes) is on the -Y side, the compact heel on +Y.

THE AXIS MAPPING, verified by exporting a known mesh and reading the bytes back:

     file = ( blender.x , blender.z , blender.y )     -- NO sign flips on any axis

  So file z IS Blender Y, same sign. There is no hidden negation to account for; the convention
  statement was simply wrong.

MEASURED SURFACES at the chest centre line (|x| < 8, z 105-135), Blender Y:

     byzantine war garment, front .......  -20.57
     male body, own chest wall ..........  -15.75
     breastplate as FIRST exported ......  -12.52

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE SAMPLE - it was never reversed, it was BURIED. At -12.52 the shell sat
behind the body's own chest wall at -15.75, i.e. inside the torso, so it rendered as nothing. That
absence was read as "facing backwards" and a 180-degree rotation was prescribed. The rotation moved
it from invisible to genuinely on the character's back, which is worse and harder to diagnose.

  Diagnostic rule: a piece that does not appear is more often INSIDE the body than reversed. Compare
  its front-most surface against the BODY's chest wall before concluding anything about facing. If
  the piece is behind the body surface, no rotation will help - it needs translating forward.

PLACEMENT RULE for anything worn on the torso: put the shell's front-most surface about 1.4 units
in front of the garment it must clear, not in front of the body. The garment is the outer surface,
and on this byzantine dress it is 4.8 units proud of the body itself.

BLENDER VIEWPORT TRAP - Numpad 1 ("Front") looks along +Y and therefore shows the character's BACK.
A correctly built breastplate looks wrong there. Use Ctrl+Numpad 1 ("Back") to see the front. This
cost a full round trip: a screenshot from Numpad 1 was read as proof the piece faced the right way.

CURRENT STATE of custom_breastplate_01 - placement hand-tuned in Blender 2026-08-27 and shipped:

     object xform   loc (-0.820, -3.869, -8.924)  rot (-0.060, 0, 0)  scale (0.998, 1.060, 1.116)
     dims           39.64 wide x 26.40 deep x 38.36 tall
     bbox (Blender) x -20.64..19.00   y -22.14..4.26   z 108.31..146.67
     in file space  x -20.64..19.00   y 108.31..146.67   z -22.14..4.26
     rotation       a ~3.4-degree forward tilt about X - a plate hangs off the chest, it is not
                    vertical, and the tilt is what stops the lower edge digging into the stomach

  The transform was baked before export (transform_apply), verified by asserting the world bbox was
  identical before and after. Bake before exporting: it makes local == world so every later
  measurement means one thing.

  NOTE the top reaches z 146.67, ABOVE the rig's neck joint at 142.8. That is deliberate - it has to
  clear bulky collars - but it is the first thing to drop ~4 units if the straps intersect a head.

  Scale width and depth freely if a piece reads small. Be careful scaling height: the band between
  lumbar (100.9) and shoulder (135.7) is fixed by the skeleton, and a taller plate rides up the neck.

ONE REFERENCE GARMENT IS NOT A FIT TARGET. Fitting was done against
m_clothes_sec_ep3_byzantine_era2_war_nob_01, and against THAT the plate cleared everywhere by
1.4-4.5 units - while still reading as buried in game, because the character on screen was wearing a
far bulkier eastern robe. Garment bulk at the chest varies a lot between cultures. Fit against the
BULKIEST war garment you expect the piece to appear over, not a typical one.


5C. VERTEX GROUP NAMING IS NOT CONSISTENT ACROSS VANILLA GARMENTS
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Relevant to the rename blocker in 5, because it decides what you are renaming FROM.

Weights are transferred from a vanilla garment (see 5D), and the group names come with them. Those
names differ between garments:

     m_clothes_religious_african_hi_01 ......  male_body_rig_01_body_root   (prefixed)
     m_clothes_sec_ep3_byzantine_era2_war_nob_01 ....  body_root            (bare)

Transferring from the byzantine garment produced 134 bare group names, and io_pdx_mesh refused the
export outright:

     Mesh ...Shape has vertices skinned to a group (body_root) targeting a missing or excluded
     armature bone!

which is at least a loud failure rather than a silent one. Re-prefix to match whichever armature the
mesh is actually bound to before exporting. If the fix for the vanilla-name mismatch is done on the
C# side as 5 suggests, it must tolerate BOTH input spellings, or handle whichever one it sees and
say so when it sees neither.


5D. WEIGHTING: TRANSFER, NEVER AUTOMATIC
-----------------------------------------

io_pdx_mesh imports joints as bones roughly 0.1-0.5 units long on a 150-unit figure. They are
positioned correctly but have no meaningful length, so "With Automatic Weights" - which works off
bone envelopes - has nothing to work from and produces garbage.

Use a Data Transfer modifier from a vanilla garment instead:

     object              the vanilla garment
     use_vert_data       True
     data_types_verts    {'VGROUP_WEIGHTS'}
     vert_mapping        'POLYINTERP_NEAREST'
     layers_vgroup_src   ALL        layers_vgroup_dst   NAME

First try on the breastplate: 134 groups, 0 unweighted vertices, influences anatomically sensible
(thoracic 2853, lumbar 1546, body_root 792, deltoids next).

RE-TRANSFER AFTER ANY TRANSFORM. Weights are bound to vertices, not to positions, so rotating or
moving the mesh leaves each vertex holding its old bone. A 180-degree turn about the vertical is the
dangerous one: it swaps left and right, so the left shoulder bone ends up driving the right side.
Nothing errors, and it only shows in animation.


5E. TEXTURES: THE PROPERTIES MAP IS PACKED, NOT A PHOTO
--------------------------------------------------------

This caused the black patches on the first in-game render, and it is worth understanding because
nothing about it looks wrong in Blender.

  CORRECTED 2026-08-27. An earlier version of this section had the channels wrong, having
  generalised from ONE garment. The authoritative packing, from Owl's portrait-modding thread
  and confirmed against 8 more vanilla maps:

      channel   what it is       vanilla cloth
      R         (unused, black)  0 on 8 of 8 measured
      G         (unused, black)  0 on 8 of 8 measured
      B         METALNESS        0.00 - 0.13 for cloth; vanilla banners 0.35 - 0.71
      A         ROUGHNESS        0.33 - 0.78

  m_clothes_sec_ep3_byzantine_era2_war_nob_01 measures G = 0.32 and is the ONE outlier in nine
  files. It was the file this section was originally written from. Sample several before you
  generalise.

  What was shipped first, by writing a greyscale roughness photo into all three channels:

      R         mean 0.62, max 0.87

  R is metalness, so the engine was told the breastplate was ~62% metal. A metallic surface with no
  environment reflection to catch, under portrait lighting, renders BLACK. The parts that did show
  texture were simply where that photo happened to be dark.

  DIAGNOSTIC: black patches are a TEXTURE problem, not a depth problem. A buried piece is invisible -
  you see the garment through it. A piece that is present but black is being lit wrongly. Do not
  chase geometry for this.

  Build the map by channel, do not paste a photo. One line, ImageMagick 7:

      magick ( -size 2048x2048 xc:black ) ( -size 2048x2048 xc:black ) ( -size 2048x2048 xc:"gray(153)" ) ( rough.jpg -resize 2048x2048 -colorspace Gray ) -channel RGBA -combine -colorspace sRGB -define dds:compression=dxt5 -define dds:mipmaps=11 out_properties.dds

        arg 1 -> R = black
        arg 2 -> G = black
        arg 3 -> B = METALNESS   gray(153) = 0.60, a steel-ish value
        arg 4 -> A = ROUGHNESS   the only channel carrying photographic detail
      Note -channel RGBA: without it -combine only fills RGB and the alpha is lost.
      In bash the parentheses need escaping as \( and \) ; in PowerShell they do not.

  B is the metal lever. Prove the piece renders at B = 0 first, then raise it, so a black result
  has only one possible cause.

  THE NORMAL MAP IS ALSO PACKED, and its blue channel is EMISSIVE, not normal Z:

      normal RGBA = R R E G    R = Normal.X, G = Normal.X, B = EMISSIVE, A = Normal.Y

  A bump or height photo dropped in whole therefore makes the piece GLOW. Neutral flat normal is
  0.502 / 0.502 / 0 / 0.502, which is exactly what vanilla measures. Normals are +Y (OpenGL).

  In Maya the properties map plugs into the Specular Color node; in Blender with io_pdx_mesh it
  goes into the Principled BSDF ROUGHNESS socket, which the exporter writes as `spec`.

  Source for the packing: forum.paradoxplaza.com, "[RESOURCE] Owl's CK3 Portrait Modding corner",
  post #1. It is more precise than the CK3 wiki's 3D_models page, which disagrees and is wrong.

OTHER TEXTURE NOTES

  * DDS conversion: ImageMagick is on this machine and writes DXT5 with mipmaps. CK3 will not read
    jpg or png, so conversion is mandatory, and there is no Blender-side export for it.
  * alpha_to_coverage uses the DIFFUSE ALPHA for coverage. Source jpgs have no alpha; ImageMagick
    fills it opaque (verified mean = min = max = 1), which is what you want. If a piece ever renders
    partly transparent, check the diffuse alpha first - that is the channel that would do it.
  * Only the BASENAME is written into the .mesh, and CK3 resolves textures globally by filename.
    Nothing needs to sit beside the mesh, but keeping it adjacent matches vanilla.

5G. BLEND SHAPES - THE REAL COST, AND THE EXPORT TRAP
------------------------------------------------------

A worn garment does not deform to body type on its own. Every body shape needs its own MESH,
listed on the entity as an attribute:

    attribute = { name = "bs_body_fat_1"  blend_shape = "<garment>_bs_fat" }

COUNT (from Owl, post #23):
    male    6   fat, infant, gaunt, old, muscular, dwarf
    female  9   the same six PLUS breast_min_size, breast_max_size, pregnant

So a piece shipped for both genders is 15 extra meshes, not 12. Vanilla bears this out: 2614
of 2960 clothing meshes under m_clothes/f_clothes are _bs_ variants - about 88% of the files.

MAKING THEM is not hard, just laborious: duplicate the base mesh and move vertices to fit each
extreme body. The vanilla body meshes for each shape are in the game files to fit against, so
it is automatable with a shrinkwrap-and-correct pass.

** EXPORT TRAP - UNTICK "MERGE VERTICES" **
   Blendshapes are matched by VERTEX ORDER, not by name or position. Vanilla meshes have
   overlapping verts along seams; letting the exporter merge them renumbers every vertex ID and
   the blendshape comes out, in Owl's words, "a jumbled mess". In Blender's io_pdx_mesh this is
   the "skip merging vertices" option - tick it.

** Blendshape meshes export MESH ONLY - no skeleton. **

Our custom_breastplate_01 has NO blendshapes, which is why it stays rigid while the character
changes weight. That is expected, not a bug, and it is the next piece of work.


5H. HEAD AND BODY ARE SEPARATE SKELETONS
-----------------------------------------

Anything attached to the head cannot take body skin weights, and vice versa. A helmet is fine, a
gorget or a high collar that spans the neck is not - it will clip whichever skeleton it is not
weighted to, no matter how carefully it is painted. Plan pieces so they sit clearly on one side
of the neck line.

This also makes a helmet the EASIEST first custom piece: the skull does not change across the
body blendshapes, so a helmet needs far fewer variants than a torso piece.

5I. TEXTURE AUTHORING - CHANNEL PACKING AND THE BAKE
-----------------------------------------------------

CHANNEL PACKING. From Owl's portrait-modding thread, verified against vanilla:

     diffuse      RGB = colour,  A = opacity
     normal       RGBA = X  X  Emissive  Y      (+Y / OpenGL, NOT DirectX)
     properties   RGBA = -  -  Metallic  Roughness

  So R and G of the properties map must be BLACK. Measured across 8 vanilla clothing
  properties maps: R=0 and G=0 on every one, metallic 0..0.13, roughness 0.33..0.78.
  Vanilla banners run metallic 0.35..0.71, so metal does render - cloth is simply not metal.
  A neutral normal map is 0.502 / 0.502 / 0 / 0.502.

  GETTING THIS WRONG LOOKS LIKE LIGHTING DAMAGE, NOT A TEXTURE BUG. Feeding a greyscale
  roughness photo into all three channels put 0.62 in R and G and lit the piece as
  emissive/metallic - it read as "the model is broken" rather than "the map is packed wrong".

BAKING A TILING MATERIAL INTO A GARMENT'S UV LAYOUT (Cycles):
  1. Material samples the source through Texture Coordinate -> Object -> Mapping, with the
     Image Texture node set projection='BOX', projection_blend ~0.3. Scale 0.09 gives about
     one fabric tile per 11 world units on a 145-unit body.
  2. Make map1 the active UV layer - that is the bake target space.
  3. Add an Image Texture node holding the destination image and make it the ACTIVE node.
     That is what marks it as the bake target; nothing else does.
  4. bake(type='DIFFUSE') with use_pass_direct=False and use_pass_indirect=False, then
     'ROUGHNESS', then 'NORMAL'. Margin 8.

  TWO CALIBRATIONS, both found by measuring rather than by looking:
    * Blender bakes LINEAR values. Read back as sRGB the diffuse came out at 0.15 mean.
      Gamma 2.2 brings it to 0.32.
    * Even corrected, 0.32 is far darker than vanilla clothing, which sits at 0.72..0.91.
      A further 2.1x linear multiply reached 0.61 with no clipping. CHECK YOUR DIFFUSE
      MEAN AGAINST VANILLA before shipping - portrait lighting is dim and a "correct"
      texture reads as near-black.


5J. BLEND SHAPES - DERIVE THEM, DO NOT SCULPT THEM
---------------------------------------------------

The single biggest cost in custom clothing, and most of it is avoidable.

WHAT IT COSTS IF YOU SCULPT. Vanilla ships 2614 _bs_ variant meshes against 346 base
clothing meshes - ~88% of all clothing files are body-shape correctives. AGOT, which fits
garments independently, hand-sculpts ~5.8 correctives per garment: 1240 corrective meshes
for 213 base garments. AGOT pays a SECOND manual pass on top, tagging each accessory
set_tags = "shrink_arms,shrink_chest,shrink_belly,..." so vanilla's gene_shrink_body
deflates the body wherever the garment would clip it.

REQUIRED SHAPES:  male   fat, gaunt, muscular, old, dwarf, infant                 (6)
                  female the same plus breast_min_size, breast_max_size, pregnant (9)

THE KEY FACT. The body's own morph targets ship as separate meshes in
gfx/models/portraits/male_body/blendshapes/ and ALL SHARE THE BASE BODY'S VERTEX ORDER.
Verified: male_body.mesh and every male_bs_body_*.mesh are 3206 verts / 5486 tris. The
deformation is large - the fat morph moves vertices up to 12.28 units.

METHOD A - DERIVED GARMENT  (proven, working in game, zero sculpting)
  Build the garment FROM the body mesh and the morphs become arithmetic:

      garment_morph[i] = garment_neutral[i] + ( body_morph[i] - body_neutral[i] )

  Steps that worked for gambeson_01:
    1. Copy male_body.mesh. Offset every vertex along its own normal by 0.5.
       (Vanilla deflates the body under clothing by mean 0.147 / p90 0.568 / max 2.088,
       concentrated at the shoulders, so ~0.5 is the right order. Use 1.0-1.5 for
       something padded like a quilted gambeson.)
    2. Build all seven meshes at FULL body resolution first.
    3. ONLY THEN delete the regions the garment does not cover, deleting the SAME vertex
       indices from all seven so correspondence survives. Hands and feet are identifiable
       by dominant bone: "fi_*" = fingers (654 verts), "ftToe"/"ftBall" = toes (332).
    4. Weights, map1 UVs and the rig all come free - they are the body's.

  This CANNOT clip the body, because it is the body. No shrink_* tags needed at all.

METHOD B - DELTA TRANSFER  (untested, for meshes that are not body-derived)
  A standoff piece - breastplate, pauldron, flared skirt - does not share body topology,
  so Method A does not apply directly. The deltas can still be transferred by PROXIMITY
  rather than by index: for each garment vertex find the nearest point on the body
  surface and apply that point's interpolated morph delta. Same trick as the Data
  Transfer weight step in 5D, applied to positions instead of weights, and scriptable.
  Expect it to need a shrink_* tag or a hand pass where a piece stands far off the body
  and the interpolated delta stops being meaningful. NOT YET TESTED.

  The split: derive the close layer, delta-transfer or sculpt the standoff layer.

EXPORT RULES FOR BLEND SHAPE MESHES
  * MESH ONLY - chk_skel=False. The base carries the skeleton and skin; the morph meshes
    must carry neither. Verified in the shipped files: base 134 bones + skin block,
    morphs 0 bones and no skin.
  * DO NOT merge vertices. Blend shapes match by vertex ORDER; merging renumbers them.
  * Every morph must match the base's vertex and triangle count exactly. Assert it.
  * Put them in a blendshapes/ subfolder - vanilla's own convention.

WIRING (gambeson_01.asset is a working example)
    pdxmesh = {
        blend_shape = { id = "<name>_bs_fat"  type = "blendshapes/<name>_bs_fat_1.mesh" }
    }
    entity = {
        attribute = { name = "bs_body_fat_1"  blend_shape = "<name>_bs_fat" }
    }
  The `attribute name` is the contract - it says "drive my shape with whatever weight the
  body's bs_body_fat_1 currently has". The blend_shape id is yours to choose; the
  attribute name is not.


5K. gambeson_01 - THE WORKED EXAMPLE
-------------------------------------
Confirmed working in game, blend shapes deforming correctly.

  source        derived from male_body.mesh, +0.5 along normals, hands and feet removed
  geometry      3852 tris, 2220 verts (11556 split), 134 bones, map1 + map2
  morphs        6, all derived, all verified identical vert/tri counts
  textures      ambientCG Fabric063 (CC0), box-projected object-space, baked into map1
  shader        portrait_attachment  - deliberately NOT the _pattern_ variant

  IT USES THE PLAIN SHADER ON PURPOSE. The pattern shader samples a pattern_mask and a
  variation; supply neither and the piece renders black (see 5H). To recolour it
  procedurally, add a portrait_accessory block with pattern_mask + variation first.

  A base layer like this belongs in the CLOTHES gene, not cloaks - it is a replacement
  outfit. That is the point: it frees the single cloak slot for a standoff piece, and it
  removes garment-vs-body clipping at source instead of tagging around it.


5L. WHY A CUSTOM PIECE RENDERS BLACK
-------------------------------------
Cost several rounds. In order of likelihood:

  1. THE PATTERN MASK IS SAMPLED IN UV0 (= map1), AND IT IS THE GARMENT'S MASK, NOT YOURS.
     The generated entity inherits pattern_mask from the vanilla garment it is modelled
     on. That mask is authored for the garment's UV layout; sampled through a custom
     piece's unrelated map1 it lights arbitrary channels, and
     Diffuse.rgb *= PatternProperties.rrr then drives whole regions to zero.
     TELL: black on clothed characters but correctly textured on an unclothed one, and
     irregular islands of visible texture rather than uniform black.
     FIX: author a mask in your own map1 space (a solid R=1 image assigns the whole piece
     to palette channel 0) and point pattern_mask at it - or drop pattern_mask and
     variation and use shader = "portrait_attachment".
  2. PROPERTIES MAP PACKED WRONG - see 5E. Non-zero R/G lights the piece as metal.
  3. Textures genuinely not loading. Check the DDS header matches vanilla: 'DDS ' magic,
     DXT5 fourcc, pfflags 0x4. ImageMagick's DXT5 output is accepted by the engine.

  DIAGNOSTIC ORDER MATTERS: a piece that is INVISIBLE is usually buried inside the body
  (5B); a piece that is BLACK is usually a shader/mask fault. Different faults, different
  fixes, and they are easy to confuse.


6. THE END GOAL: ARMOUR ASSEMBLED FROM LAYERED PIECES
-----------------------------------------------------
Proven as of 2026-08-27: a body-derived base garment (gambeson_01, clothes slot) and a hard piece
over it (custom_breastplate_01, cloaks slot) render TOGETHER on one character, overriding the
clothing underneath. That is the two-layer stack the whole idea rests on, and it works.

** THE LAYERING BUDGET IS THREE, NOT TWO. **
   The constraint that decides how far this can go is how many gene slots attach at the torso, and
   it is more generous than the clothes/cloaks pair suggests. Counted over every vanilla accessory
   carrying `shared_pose_entity = torso` (771 of them), grouped by the gene each belongs to:

       clothes           626      the base layer; REPLACES the outfit
       legwear            61      also torso-attached, despite the name
       cloaks             54      LAYERS over clothes
       special_legwear     2

   So a character can wear a base, a torso overlay AND a leg piece simultaneously, with helmets
   arriving separately through the headgear genes. Anatomically that lines up better than it has any
   right to: gambeson underneath, cuirass over it, tassets or greaves on the legwear slot.

   Untested: whether legwear layers over clothes the way cloaks do, or replaces like clothes. That
   is the first thing to establish, because it decides whether the budget is three layers or two
   plus a substitution.

** RECOLOURING THE BASE IS ALREADY BUILT. **
   Making the gambeson read as mail, or as any material, needs no new machinery — only a
   `portrait_accessory` block on its entity naming a pattern_mask and a variation, which is exactly
   what ArmorForgeStep already emits for vanilla garments, palette and all. The piece ships map1 and
   map2, so it can take a swatch. What it does NOT yet ship is a mask; the breastplate does
   (custom_breastplate_01_masks.dds, R=1 across the plate).

** WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY BE HARD. **
   Not the plumbing - that is all proven now. The hard parts are:
     - Seams. Two rigid pieces meeting on a deforming body is a much worse visual problem than a
       seam on a rigid sword, and it is the thing the weapon forge never had to solve.
     - Per-piece morphs. Every hand-modelled piece needs its own blend shapes or it stays rigid
       while the body changes. The body-derivation trick makes those free for close-fitting pieces
       and does nothing for a piece that is not body-shaped.
     - Combinatorics. A base x an overlay x a leg piece x a palette is a lot of emitted text, and
       every combination needs its own accessory, entity and template entry.


7. WHAT WOULD COME NEXT
-----------------------
Custom armour meshes are the natural next step now that recolouring works, and the path is shorter
than it looks: a custom garment needs the same five files as a vanilla one, and only the entity's
`pdxmesh` changes. The catalogue would gain a source of garments beyond vanilla's 75.

Open questions, in the order they would bite:
  - Does the bone rename actually make it bind? Nothing has been in the engine yet.
  - What is `sfs` for?
  - Do custom garments need the blend-shape attributes (bs_body_fat_1, _gaunt, _muscular, _old,
    _dwarf, _infant) that vanilla entities carry? Our emitted entities copy them verbatim from the
    vanilla entity precisely so a garment follows a fat or dwarf body. A custom mesh has no blend
    shapes, so it would be rigid across body types — probably visible, probably needs authoring.
  - Vanilla garments set tags (`shrink_arms,shrink_chest,shrink_belly,short_skirt`) that shrink the
    body underneath so it does not poke through. A custom piece will need its own.


8. HOW TO TEST
--------------
The debug menu autopopulates from events. Two are relevant:
  Debug: Forge a Weapon    weapons, one option per kind
  Debug: Forge Armour      one option per armour TYPE, so plate and mail can be compared directly

Mint plate, then mail, on the same character — those are the furthest apart. Then hand one to a
ruler of another culture and confirm it does NOT change: that is the creator gate, and it is the
requirement most likely to break quietly.

And read error.log. Three assumptions about this feature have been wrong so far — visual caching,
template membership, gene merging — and tiger passed all three. The log caught every one.


9. PIPELINE: TAKING A DOWNLOADED ARMOUR SET INTO CK3
-----------------------------------------------------
Written 2026-08-28 from doing it end to end with DevonLux's "5 Piece Platemail" (CC-BY, Sketchfab).
Every number here was measured on this machine. The order below is the order the traps appear in.

9A. SOURCING
  CC0 HAS NO LIMB ARMOUR. Searched downloadable CC0 on Sketchfab for pauldron, gauntlet, vambrace,
  greave, sabaton, tasset, cuisse, sallet, armet, barbute, backplate, chainmail: ZERO results for
  every one. CC0 yields helmets and breastplates only (Wirtualne Muzea Malopolski, Cleveland
  Museum of Art). A full harness is reachable under CC-BY or not at all.
  Best CC-BY sources found: DevonLux (6 MetaHuman-rigged sets, pre-split into slots),
  ernst52 (historically accurate harnesses plus 8 helmets), Multipainkiller_Studio (already at
  game polycount, 1-7k tris).
  Sketchfab's API answers this far faster than the web UI: api.sketchfab.com/v3/search
  ?type=models&downloadable=true&license=<slug>. Slugs come from /v3/licenses - it is "by",
  not "cc-by", and a wrong slug returns zero for everything rather than erroring.

9B. ** THE IMPORTED RIG IS BROKEN UNLESS YOU TURN OFF THE BIND-POSE GUESS **
  Sketchfab auto-converts uploads to glTF and the skeleton loses fidelity. Imported with defaults,
  283 of 342 bones collapsed onto ONE point (83%); the other three armature copies were 42-91%
  collapsed. The mesh still DEFORMS CORRECTLY because the pose carries it, so nothing looks wrong
  until you try to retarget and the maths returns garbage.

      bpy.ops.import_scene.gltf(filepath=..., guess_original_bind_pose=False)

  drops that to 2 of 342 and makes all four armature copies identical. No re-download needed.
  NOTE this changes the space of the imported data: with the guess off, meshes stay in glTF Y-up.
  Calibrate in WORLD space (obj.evaluated_get(dg), then matrix_world) and the question disappears.

9C. SCALE AND FACING - CHECK BEFORE ASSUMING A CONVERSION IS NEEDED
  MetaHuman and CK3 are almost the same scale. Neck-to-neck, MetaHuman torso top 134.97 against
  CK3's REF_body_male 145.08: a uniform 1.075x. Not a unit conversion.
  Facing: in the glTF the feet run z -5.8..+20.5, so toes lead +Z. Blender's importer maps
  +Z to -Y, and CK3 characters face -Y in Blender (5B). So a plain import already faces correctly.

9D. RETARGETING THE POSE - AIM AT JOINT POSITIONS, NOT BONE ORIENTATIONS
  io_pdx_mesh imports CK3 joints as bones 0.095-0.5 units long, so CK3 bone DIRECTIONS are
  meaningless (5D). Retarget on positions: for each (parent, child) pair, rotate the parent so the
  child's head lands on the CK3 joint it maps to; iterate the whole chain 3-4 times.
  Mapping that worked: pelvis to body_root, spine_02 to bn_sp_lumbar, spine_04 to bn_sp_thoracic,
  neck_01 to bn_sp_cervical, clavicle_l to bn_l_clavicle, upperarm_l to bn_l_shoulder,
  lowerarm_l to bn_l_elbow, hand_l to bn_l_wrist, thigh_l to bn_l_hip, calf_l to bn_l_knee,
  foot_l to bn_l_ankle. CK3 has NO head bone on the body rig - the head is a separate skeleton (5H).
  Result: cervical 6.82 to 0.32, shoulder 6.55 to 0.66, wrist 10.45 to 4.39, and arm divergence
  across the forearm went from about -12 units to plus/minus 2.
  ** LIMIT: aiming fixes DIRECTION ONLY. ** Where the source bone length differs from CK3's, no
  rotation closes the gap - the elbow got WORSE (1.31 to 2.98) and the legs barely moved. Residuals
  of 3-4 units on a 145-unit figure do not show. If they ever matter, scale bone lengths too.

9E. ** CLEAR THE SOURCE VERTEX GROUPS BEFORE TRANSFERRING WEIGHTS **
  The imported mesh arrives skinned to its own rig. Run a Data Transfer without deleting those
  groups first and you get 342 + 134 = 476 groups; the 4-influence cap then keeps the ORIGINAL
  weights over the transferred ones, because they are heavier. The gloves came out driven by 210
  bones. It does not error, and the group count is the only tell.

      d.vertex_groups.clear()      # BEFORE adding the Data Transfer modifier

  Transfer source: use the BODY (male_body.mesh), not a garment. It is the only source with full
  coverage - a robe stops at |x| 49.6 and z 8.7, so it cannot weight hands or feet, and it has the
  finger and toe groups a gauntlet or sabaton needs.
  Afterwards: vertex_group_limit_total(limit=4), then normalize_all. CK3 skins are 4 influences.
  VERIFY by dominant bone per piece - it should read anatomically (boots to ankle/ftBall, gloves to
  wrist plus fingers). That check also revealed the piece the glTF called "Chest_Knight.003" is
  actually the VAMBRACES.
  AND VERIFY LEFT/RIGHT (5D warns of this): establish the body's convention (+x maps to bn_l_*) and
  confirm every piece agrees. A mirrored binding only shows in animation.

9F. EXPORT REQUIREMENTS
  * The material needs a "shader" CUSTOM PROPERTY or io_pdx_mesh refuses outright:
    "found no meshes with PDX materials applied". The PDXmat_ name alone is not enough.
        mat["shader"] = "portrait_attachment_pattern_alpha_to_coverage"
  * Shape name comes from obj.data.name and IS the contract. Copying a piece whose datablock name
    is already taken silently yields "plate01_cuirassShape.001", which matches nothing. Rename the
    full-res masters out of the way (_src) so the shipping tier can hold the clean names.
  * chk_selected=True or it exports the whole scene; chk_split_vtx=True (both shipped pieces are
    fully split); chk_locs=False - armour parts need NO locators, unlike weapon parts, because the
    pieces are skinned into one shared body space and self-assemble at zero translation.
  VERIFY the written file against a known-good one: magic "@@b@", and the same count of distinct
  bn_* names (129) as gambeson_01.mesh.

9G. TEXTURES FROM A glTF SOURCE
  glTF packs metallicRoughness as G=roughness, B=metalness. CK3 wants "- - Metal Rough" (5I).
  So properties = [black, black, MR.blue, MR.green] and normal = [N.red, N.red, black, N.green].
  Size to match the piece - 2048, DXT5, 12 mips, like custom_breastplate_01.
  MEASURE THE RESULT against a shipped piece rather than against absolute figures. Ours came out
  properties R=0 G=0 B=53.2 A=65.4 against the breastplate's 0/0/60/62.4, and normal
  50.0/49.9/0/50.0 against 50.2/50.2/0/50.2. Diffuse luma 62.7% against vanilla war garments at
  63.4-85.6%.
  Do not judge diffuse by per-channel RGB means - measure luma, the same way, across all files.
  The textures must be wired into the Blender material as image nodes (diffuse to Base Color,
  properties to Roughness, normal to Normal Map to Normal) BEFORE export: the .mesh stores
  basenames only, and a material with no image nodes exports with none.

9H. ** A MIRRORED SOURCE MODEL HAS INVERTED WINDING ON ONE HALF **
  Symptom: one arm renders see-through in game, the other is fine. Blender draws faces double-sided
  so it looks perfect there; CK3 BACKFACE-CULLS, so the bad half renders as its own interior.
  DIAGNOSTIC that names it beyond doubt: measure the share of faces whose normal points away from
  the nearest body vertex, per side. Ours read
      vambrace  L 4.5%  R 95.2%      gloves  L 30%  R 70.6%      cuirass  L 26.7%  R 76.8%
  i.e. L is almost exactly 100 - R on every piece: a pure inversion of the left half, not noise.
  FIX: bmesh.ops.recalc_face_normals. Note this is a per-shell heuristic and it is NOT reliable on
  layered pieces - it fixed the vambrace (L to 95.5, symmetric) and the gloves, but on the cuirass
  it dropped the GOOD side from 76.8 to 51.8. On a piece with many separate lames, flipping only
  the mirrored half is safer than a global recalculate.

9I. ** OPEN SHELLS: THERE IS NO NO-CULL SHADER. IT MUST BE FIXED WITH GEOMETRY **
  Symptom: undersides of pauldrons, and any surface facing away from the camera, are invisible.
  CK3 ships 19 portrait_attachment effect variants; the feature axes are alpha, alpha_to_coverage,
  pattern, no_blend, with_coa, and_variations, selection. NONE is two-sided, and portrait.shader
  declares no rasterizer or cull state at all - culling is engine-fixed, not selectable.
  Every piece of this set was an open shell: cuirass 21.4% boundary edges, legs 36.5%, boots 33.3%,
  gloves 26.5%, vambrace 11%.
  FIX: Solidify, thickness about 0.5, offset -1.0, and ** use_even_offset = False **.
  With even-offset ON, the cuirass's 222 non-manifold edges blew the bounding box up by +124 in x
  and +70 in z; thickness_clamp does NOT rescue it. With it OFF the growth is exactly 0 on all
  three axes - the shell thickens inward, silhouette untouched - and boundary edges fall 1590 to 56.
  COST: the cuirass went 4,499 to 12,172 tris. Budget about 2.7x for any piece you solidify.

9J. TAGS: THE BODY SHRINKS, IT DOES NOT HIDE
  There is NO way to stop CK3 rendering hands. The complete body-morph vocabulary is
  shrink_{arms,belly,chest,feet,hands,legs} plus bs_body_no_portrait (the whole figure) and
  bs_body_no_left_leg (that one limb only). Hands can only be deflated.
  Measured: male_bs_body_shrink_hands moves hand vertices by median 0.68, max 2.02 units. That is
  a small correction - if fingers still poke through a gauntlet, suspect the gauntlet's decimation
  before suspecting the tag.
  Vanilla set_tags vocabulary by frequency: shrink_belly 624, shrink_chest 616, shrink_arms 600,
  snug_headgear 356, no_hair 238, short_skirt 233, mask_hat 171, offset 163, hat 128, crown 102,
  no_beard 82, enclosed_helmet 68, hood 63, cap 63, shrink_legs 60, shrink_hands 56, shrink_feet 53,
  helmet 37, fit_over_headgear 28, fit_under_crown 16, nose_guard 7, stealth 4.
  gfx/portraits/accessories/ccp5_armor.txt is the template for a full harness:
      set_tags = "shrink_arms,shrink_chest,shrink_belly,short_skirt,shrink_hands"
  CustomArmorStep currently EARNS only shrink_arms, by arm-region vertex share. A full harness
  should earn shrink_chest, shrink_belly, shrink_legs, shrink_feet and shrink_hands on the same
  measured principle - that is the fix for body-through-armour clipping at the belly and legs.

9K. WHAT IS STILL NOT DONE ON plate01_parts
  * No blend shapes. These are standoff pieces, so 5J Method A does not apply; Method B (delta
    transfer by proximity) is still untested. The set is rigid across body types.
  * The helm is unused: it is unskinned, and head gear binds to a separate skeleton (5H).
  * Only shrink_arms is emitted; see 9J.
  * The cuirass's right side may have been damaged by the global normal recalculate; see 9H.
    PM_TEST in the blend still holds the pristine-winding original if it needs rebuilding.

9L. ** EARNED TAGS ARE SHARES OF THE WHOLE, SO GEOMETRY ANYWHERE DILUTES THEM **
  CustomArmorStep decides each shrink tag from the share of a piece's vertices inside a region.
  That share is a fraction of the piece's TOTAL vertex count - so adding geometry ANYWHERE lowers
  every other region's share, even though nothing about those regions changed.

  It has already bitten once. Solidifying the cuirass (9I) added roughly 6,000 vertices, all of
  them on the torso. The arm share fell from the 42.9% recorded in 9J to 31.67%, against a 30%
  threshold; the hand share fell to 16.30% against 15%. Both tags survive, but by 1.7 and 1.3
  points respectively - and losing one is SILENT. The armour simply stops occluding that part of
  the body and the limb starts poking through again, with nothing in error.log to say why.

  Measured after the solidify and a 0.01 merge-by-distance, over 13,033 vertices:
      legs 36.4%   arms 31.7%   chest 26.7%   belly 22.5%   hands 16.3%   feet 8.0%
      thresholds:  arm 0.30   hand 0.15   chest/belly/legs 0.10   feet 0.05
  All six tags earned:
      shrink_arms,shrink_chest,shrink_belly,shrink_legs,shrink_hands,shrink_feet

  ** RE-CHECK THE SHARES AFTER ANY GEOMETRY CHANGE ** - solidify, decimate, merge by distance, or
  adding a part to the library. The arm and hand thresholds are the ones with no margin left.
  If this keeps biting, the durable fix is to measure each region against the BODY's own vertex
  count in that region - i.e. "how much of the arm is covered" - rather than against the piece's
  own total, which is what actually makes a tag earned or not.
