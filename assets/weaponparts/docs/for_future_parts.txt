================================================================================
0. THE FOUR CONTRACTS
================================================================================
Break any of these and the failure is silent or misleading, not a clean error.

  A. NAMING     mesh datablock is exactly  <family>_<slot>Shape
                slots are exactly: blade, guard, grip, pommel
                Export reads obj.data.name, NOT the object name.

  B. SOCKETS    6 empties per family, named <family>_<slot>_socket_<target>
                A mating pair MUST sit at the identical point.

  C. TEXTURES   All three maps resolve to real files on disk, sourced from the
                weapon's .asset (never from the .mesh). See section 5.

  D. GEOMETRY   Never move, rotate or scale part geometry. Only add empties.
                The assembler translates and nothing else; it does not transform
                normals or tangents, so any baked rotation gives wrong lighting.

  E. UV2        Exactly two UV layers, "map1" (untouched) then "map2" (new), in
                that order. map2 is a world-space box projection at ONE global
                scale shared by the whole library. See section 5B.

  F. THE GENERATOR NEVER SEES BLENDER. It assembles in C# from the .mesh alone,
     so NAMING IS THE ENTIRE CONTRACT and every failure below is silent:
       - a family missing a REQUIRED slot is dropped from the pool, no message;
       - a shape whose suffix matches no slot is skipped without comment;
       - suffix matching is case-insensitive;
       - PARENTING DOES NOT SURVIVE EXPORT. No parent or transform data is
         written, so the NAME is the only thing binding a socket to its part.
         Rename a part and you must rename its locators in the same pass.

  G. UV2 IS CHECKED BATCH-WIDE. ONE part missing map2 disables recolouring for
     ALL generated weapons and their rendered icons, not just its own family.
     The run prints a line saying so, but the damage is far wider than the cause.
     This is the cheapest thing to get wrong and the most expensive to miss.

Contract D refined 2026-08-27 (additive repair): D forbids MOVING, ROTATING or
SCALING existing geometry. ADDING faces to repair a visually broken part is
permitted, provided (a) no existing vertex moves and (b) the part's bbox is
unchanged - those two conditions are exactly what keeps its sockets valid. Done
once so far: ep1_western_hammer_01_a_head, see section 11.

Family names must be globally unique AND humanly distinct. The C# side matches
families exactly (no substring matching), so a typo fails loudly - but two
families a reader confuses is still a bug. Seven of the current fourteen contain
"western". Before exporting, print the family list and read it.


================================================================================
0B. SLOT TAXONOMY BY WEAPON CLASS
================================================================================
Two families of layout. The ANCHOR is the part the hand holds and the part every
other part is placed relative to; the LEAD is the business end.

  class          slots                              anchor   lead
  -------------  ---------------------------------  -------  ------
  sword, dagger  blade guard grip pommel            grip     blade
                 ALL FOUR REQUIRED
  axe, mace      head haft [cap]                    haft     head
                 head+haft required, cap OPTIONAL

`cap` is genuinely optional - half the vanilla axes have no butt cap, and
forcing one produces a degenerate part. See section 3.

SOCKETS - one per side of every join, so a pair is TWO empties at ONE point:

  bladed (6 per family)          hafted (4, or 6 with a cap)
    grip:   socket_guard           haft: socket_head
            socket_pommel                socket_cap    (only if it has a cap)
    guard:  socket_grip            head: socket_haft
            socket_blade           cap:  socket_haft
    pommel: socket_grip
    blade:  socket_guard

  Chain, bladed:  grip -> guard -> blade,  grip -> pommel
  Chain, hafted:  haft -> head,            haft -> cap

Names: shapes are <family>_<slot>Shape on the MESH DATABLOCK; locators are
<family>_<slot>_socket_<target> on the OBJECT. The family is literally
everything before _<slot>Shape, so all parts of one weapon must share a
byte-identical prefix.

================================================================================
1. PICK THE RIGHT SOURCE MESH
================================================================================
Use the *portrait* variant. Always.

  GOOD  gfx/models/artifacts/weapons/<name>_portrait.mesh
  GOOD  gfx/models/artifacts/props/**/<name>_portrait.mesh
  BAD   gfx/models/artifacts/artifacts/<name>.mesh

The non-portrait meshes are display-stand models. tgp_japanese_sword_01_a.mesh
imports the katana PLUS ep1_western_weaponrack_01 as a second object. The
portrait variant is the bare weapon.

Find candidates with:
  find "<CK3>/game/gfx/models/artifacts" -ipath "*portrait*" -iname "*sword*.mesh"

NAME YOUR SEARCH PATTERNS EXHAUSTIVELY. A first spear sweep used spear/lance/
pike/glaive/halberd/javelin/trident/naginata/yari and MISSED the glaive entirely,
because vanilla calls it "polearm". The conclusion "no portrait variant exists"
was wrong and would have cost the best spear in the set. When a search comes back
empty, suspect the pattern before you suspect the game. Cross-check by grepping
the .asset files for shader = "portrait_attachment" instead of guessing filenames.

A NON-PORTRAIT SOURCE IS SOMETIMES USABLE. ep2_lance_01_a has no portrait variant
but lives in props/, imports bare (no rack) and is already on the +Y convention,
so it was taken. Probe before rejecting: import it and look at what came in.

RE-ORIENTING A SOURCE IS ALLOWED, AT IMPORT ONLY. tgp_chinese_polearm_01 is
authored along Z instead of Y. Rotating a whole source once, BEFORE cutting, with
transform_apply(rotation=True), is safe: a pure rotation has determinant +1 so
winding is preserved, normals rotate with the mesh, and tangents are recomputed
from UVs at export. This does NOT contradict contract D, which forbids rotating
PARTS after authoring, where a rotated part would no longer match its neighbours.
In the end no rotation was needed - the _02 portrait variant is already correct -
and staying byte-faithful to vanilla is the better outcome when you have the choice.

================================================================================
3. CUT INTO PARTS
================================================================================
The PDX exporter never welds components, so parts are already separate islands.
Nearly every sword is 15-40 loose parts.

Procedure:
  1. Separate by loose parts (mesh.separate type='LOOSE').
  2. For each island compute the centroid along Y (Y is the blade axis in
     Blender; tip at negative Y, pommel at positive Y).
  3. Classify with THREE cut planes on that centroid:
        blade  <  c1  <=  guard  <  c2  <=  grip  <  c3  <=  pommel
  4. Join each group, name it <family>_<slot>, set data.name to that + "Shape".
  5. Assert total faces out == total faces in. This catches a dropped island.
  6. LOOK AT IT. Colour the four parts differently, set viewport shading to
     Object colour, and view from RIGHT orthographic. Every mistake found so far
     was found this way, not by numbers.

Read c1/c2/c3 off the island table per sword - they are not universal. Dump each
island's face count, Y range and X/Z width, then pick planes in the gaps.

WHEN A SWORD CANNOT BE CUT
  Some hilts are modelled as ONE island spanning guard+grip (talwar-style, with
  a disc pommel and integral knuckle guard). Loose-part splitting cannot
  separate those. Do not force it with a bisect - that creates new geometry and
  changes topology. Exclude the sword from the library instead.

CUTTING A HAFTED WEAPON (axe, mace)
  Three slots means TWO cut planes on island centroid Y:
      head  <  c1  <=  haft  <  c2  <=  cap
  Read c1/c2 off the island table as for swords. Two extra rules:

  1. A LONG, ROUND-SECTIONED ISLAND IS THE HAFT wherever its centroid falls.
     Some hafts are modelled in SEGMENTS that interleave with the head, so the
     lower segment's centroid sits deep in head territory and a pure centroid
     rule files it as head. Override with:
         y_extent > 20 and 0.7 < zw/xw < 1.5  ->  haft
     Verified safe across all 8 axes and 10 maces: it catches exactly the
     segmented hafts (mena axe, mena/african/steppe/byzantine maces) and never
     grabs a head, because heads are Z-wide (ratio 3-25) or short.

  1B. THE OVERRIDE ABOVE IS AXE/MACE ONLY - DO NOT USE IT ON SPEARS.
     ep1_western_spear_01_a's blade is FOUR islands each 29.44 units long with a
     near-circular section (xw 1.58, zw 1.56, ratio 0.99). It satisfies
     "y_extent > 20 and 0.7 < ratio < 1.5" perfectly and would be filed as haft.
     Spear blades are long and round; axe heads are short and Z-wide. The
     heuristic encodes an axe-shaped assumption and does not transfer.
     Spears were cut with pure centroid planes and needed no override.

  2. A SUB-0.1-THICK END DISC IS NOT A CAP. It is the flat face closing the
     haft tube. Fold it into the haft and let the family be two-slot.
     Measured: northern axe 12 faces / 0.075 thick, steppe axe 14 / 0.086.
     Shipping those as `cap` gives a degenerate part with a meaningless socket
     cross-section. Half the vanilla axes genuinely have no butt cap.

PARTS THAT RUN ALONGSIDE OTHER PARTS
  Knuckle-bows, langets and side-rings belong to the GUARD functionally but sit
  along the grip's whole length, so a centroid rule files them under grip. Move
  them to the guard explicitly. This matters for socket placement - see 4.


================================================================================
4. PLACE THE SOCKETS
================================================================================
Six empties per family:

  grip    socket_guard    socket_pommel
  guard   socket_grip     socket_blade
  blade   socket_guard
  pommel  socket_grip

The grip is the anchor; everything is placed relative to it.
Chain: grip -> guard -> blade, and grip -> pommel.
Rule: A's socket_B aligns onto B's socket_A, so a mating pair sits at ONE point.

--- DEPTH (Y) ------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchor the join to the TERMINATING part's end face, then push in by half the
overlap:

  guard <-> blade   blade terminates at its ymax   -> yj = blade.ymax - d/2
  grip  <-> guard   grip  terminates at its ymin   -> yj = grip.ymin  + d/2
  grip  <-> pommel  grip  terminates at its ymax   -> yj = grip.ymax  - d/2
  haft  <-> head    haft  terminates at its ymin   -> yj = haft.ymin  + d/2
  haft  <-> cap     haft  terminates at its ymax   -> yj = haft.ymax  - d/2

THE PRINCIPLE, AND THE BUG IT PREVENTS (added 2026-08-27):

  ANCHOR ON THE ROD'S END - the part that passes THROUGH - NEVER on the
  FITTING'S END - the part with the cavity.

  join              rod    fitting   anchored on
  ----------------  -----  --------  ------------
  guard <-> blade   blade  guard     blade's top    (the tang)
  grip  <-> guard   grip   guard     grip's base
  grip  <-> pommel  grip   pommel    grip's top
  haft  <-> cap     haft   cap       haft's top
  haft  <-> head    haft   head      haft's TIP   <-- was inverted, see below

Only ONE join in the whole set ever had rod and fitting the wrong way round:
axe/mace haft<->head, first authored anchored on the HEAD's top. Because head
mounting depth varies enormously between families - measured 0.51 to 30.80 on
axes, 0.00 to 26.42 on maces - a foreign head then sat metres off. Re-anchoring
on the haft tip took the cross-family seating error from mean 9.03 / worst 29.55
down to mean 0.19 / worst 0.75 on axes, improving all 56 pairs and regressing
none. Swords and daggers were already rod-anchored and needed no change.

HOW TO TELL YOU HAVE IT RIGHT. Ask "does a FOREIGN rod's end land where this
fitting's own rod ended?" - the fitting owns the cavity, so its expectation is
what governs. Measured across all four libraries after the fix, worst case:
swords 0.95, daggers 1.00, axes 0.75, maces 1.00. Anything over ~1.5 is wrong.

Do NOT measure it the other way round ("does the rod get ITS native seating?").
That asks the wrong question and reports false alarms - it flagged swords at
worst 4.85 when they were in fact correct.

where d = the bbox overlap between the two parts, CAPPED at

  d = min(raw_overlap, 2.0, 0.10 * terminating_part_length)

The cap is the important part. A part that runs alongside another (that
knuckle-bow again) produces a huge bbox overlap that is not a mating surface.
Without the cap, ep1_mena_sword_01_a's grip<->guard socket landed at Y +1.74 -
the middle of the handle, 4.15 units from where it belongs. Genuine joins are
interleaves of 0.03-2.1 units and are unaffected by the cap.

Do NOT place the socket on the outer face. The overlap is deliberate - the
tang runs through the guard - and preserving it is what stops assembled weapons
showing seams.

--- CENTRING (X and Z) ---------------------------------------------------------
CORRECTED 2026-08-26 - the earlier version of this rule shipped a bug in
axe_parts.mesh. Read the whole subsection.

  A socket marks WHERE THE TWO PARTS PHYSICALLY MEET. For a part that the other
  one passes THROUGH, that is the AXIAL PART'S AXIS at the join height - NOT the
  centroid of the terminating part's cross-section.

Slice the AXIAL part at the join plane, collect where edges cross it, take the
centre of that. The axial part is the slender one - the grip, the haft, the
blade's tang - not the wide fitting it passes through.

WHY IT MATTERS. Take the cross-section of the wrong member and the socket lands
in the middle of that member's profile. For a sword this is invisible: both a
grip and a guard are centred on the weapon axis, so the two definitions coincide.
For an AXE HEAD they do not - the head hangs off one side of the haft, so its
cross-section centre sits out in the blade. Measured error on the first axe
export: head sockets 0.95 to 15.64 units off the haft axis.

SELF-REBUILD CANNOT CATCH THIS. Both members of the pair get the same wrong
point, so they still coincide and drift stays exactly 0.0. It only appears when
you mix families - an african head on a northern haft landed ~13 units aside.

Pick the axial member by SMALLEST LATERAL BBOX EXTENT, not by longest span.
ep1_african_axe_01_a's head has a long socket collar that makes it span further
along the axis than its own haft, so "longest" picks the wrong one.

For curved parts, use the axial part's section AT THE JOIN HEIGHT, not its
overall bbox centre. On the curved hafts (african, northern, chinese, western)
the two differ by up to 0.56 units.

This is the whole point of the exercise. Correctly centred sockets remove a
part's X/Z offset automatically - tgp_japanese_sword_01_a's blade sits entirely
off-axis (X spans -0.873..-0.264) and sockets fix it without re-modelling.

BEVEL TRAP: cut faces are often bevelled or capped, so the cross-section exactly
AT the join plane can be a shrunken ring or nearly degenerate, and its centre is
wrong. Sample at increasing depth into the part and take the first slice whose
area is >= 90% of the maximum seen. Report which joins needed it.
  - fired on 8 of 14 swords, 14 of 42 joins
  - worst: ep1_western_sword_04_a grip<->guard, section at the join plane was
    0.8% of full extent
  - on the katana, skipping it misplaced a socket by 2.27 units, which reads as
    "the model is a bit off" rather than as a bug

--- FLOAT32 ---------------------------------------------------------------------
Blender stores locations as float32, so a value read back differs from the
Python float you set by up to ~6e-07. That is storage, not movement. What
matters is that BOTH sockets of a pair are written from the SAME source value,
so they round identically and the translation is exactly zero. Never compute the
two halves of a pair independently.


================================================================================
5. TEXTURES  (the .asset is authoritative - never the .mesh)
================================================================================
Three families once exported with empty texture names. An untextured weapon does
not read as "missing texture" on a CK3 portrait - it reads as a HOLE PUNCHED
THROUGH THE CHARACTER.

--- WHERE THE NAMES COME FROM ---------------------------------------------------
Find the .asset declaring your mesh, then the pdxmesh block whose
  file = "<your mesh>.mesh"
then the meshsettings block for the LOD0 shape. Take:

  texture_diffuse  -> diffuse
  texture_normal   -> normal
  texture_specular -> the "_properties.dds" file    (note: properties, not spec)

TRAP 1 - the ep1_* weapons have no .asset beside them. They are declared in one
shared file: gfx/models/artifacts/weapons/ep1_artifacts_weapons_portrait.asset

TRAP 2 - a single .asset often declares SEVERAL entities. Brace-match the
pdxmesh block and match its `file =` field. Do not grep for the first
texture_diffuse: in ep2_western_longsword_01_a.asset the first one belongs to
ep1_western_weaponrack_01.

TRAP 3 - a weapon may be textured from a COMPLETELY DIFFERENT asset. The .mesh
then names a texture that lives in another folder, the importer cannot find it,
and you get a material whose image has an empty filepath - which exports as "".
ep1_northern_sword_01_a is textured with portrait_prop_fp1_marshal_sword_01_*.
This is why you go to the .asset and never trust the .mesh.

--- SLOT MAPPING (counter-intuitive) --------------------------------------------
The exporter walks three Principled BSDF inputs:

    Base Color  ->  diff  ->  texture_diffuse
    Roughness   ->  spec  ->  texture_specular   <-- the _properties.dds file
    Normal      ->  n     ->  texture_normal

THE PROPERTIES MAP GOES IN THE ROUGHNESS SLOT. Anywhere else and it is silently
not exported. Intermediate nodes are fine - the exporter follows the first
linked input upstream until it hits an Image Texture, so a Normal Map node in
the chain is no problem.

Set normal and properties images to Non-Color; diffuse to sRGB.

--- DO NOT COPY THE .dds FILES --------------------------------------------------
Only the basename is exported (os.path.split(filepath)[1]). Point each Image
Texture node straight at the real file in the CK3 game folder. CK3 resolves
textures globally by filename. The image datablock just needs a real, resolvable
filepath.

--- REUSE THE PDX MATERIAL ------------------------------------------------------
Only meshes with a PDX material are exported (it carries a "shader" custom
property). Rewire the node tree of the EXISTING material; do not create a fresh
one, or the part silently vanishes from the export.


================================================================================
5B. UV SET 2  (map2) - material space for procedural recolouring
================================================================================
This is what makes the generator's colour variations work. It is additive:
geometry, map1, materials, sockets and names all stay exactly as they are.

A SOURCE CAN SHIP MORE THAN TWO UV SETS. ep2_lance_01_a imported with map1, map2
AND map3. Writing the projection into "map2" left map3 in place, and the exporter
maps layers IN ORDER to u0,u1,u2 - so the file would have carried a third stream.
map3 turned out to be a duplicate of map1 and was deleted. ASSERT the layer list
is exactly ["map1","map2"] per part; do not merely check that map2 exists.

--- THE THING EVERYONE GETS BACKWARDS -------------------------------------------
Verified by reading the shader, not by guessing:
  jomini/gfx/FX/jomini/portrait_accessory_variation.fxh
  (note: under the jomini\ install root, NOT game\gfx\FX\jomini\)

  line 130:  float4 Mask = PdxTex2D( PatternMask, Input.UV0 );
  line 144:  ApplyPattern( Input.UV1, GetPatternDesc(...), ... );

and in jomini/gfx/FX/jomini/portrait.shader the vertex input declares UV0, then
UV1 (gated on PDX_MESH_UV1), then UV2. So:

  pattern_mask  (which channel applies where)  is sampled in UV0 = map1
  pattern swatch (the gold/bronze tiling)      is sampled in UV1 = map2

Diffuse/normal/properties also sample UV0, which is consistent - map1 is the
atlas. DO NOT try to encode "which material am I" by placing map2 islands in
quadrant regions; nothing reads map2 when sampling the mask. Channel selection
is decided by where a part's existing MAP1 islands fall.

Consequence for mixed-family weapons: within one family the four slots occupy
disjoint map1 regions (measured: zero collisions across all 14 sword families),
so a single-family weapon masks perfectly. Across families roughly half of
slot-pairs overlap in map1, so a mask must be generated per assembled weapon
from the four parts actually chosen.

--- HOW TO AUTHOR map2 ----------------------------------------------------------
map2 is a projection for TILING a material swatch. Different rules from map1:

  * Even texel density matters; packing does not. Overlapping islands are fine.
    Distortion is the enemy, wasted space is not.
  * Islands may sit anywhere, including outside 0-1. There are no regions.
  * Density MUST match across every part and every family, because the swatch
    scale comes from one shared pattern_layout. A family at twice the density
    shows visibly finer grain.

DO NOT use Smart UV Project, and do not use cube_project with scale_to_bounds.
Both rescale each island to fill 0-1, which gives every part a DIFFERENT
density - the exact failure this rule exists to prevent.

Use a pure world-space box projection at one global constant:

  SCALE = 20.0      # world units per 1.0 UV - shared by the ENTIRE library

  for each face: pick the dominant axis of the face normal, take the other two
  world coords as (u,v), divide both by SCALE.

    axis X: (u,v) = ( y, z) if n.x > 0 else (-y, z)
    axis Y: (u,v) = (-x, z) if n.y > 0 else ( x, z)
    axis Z: (u,v) = ( x, y) if n.z > 0 else ( x,-y)

Because map2 is literally world-coordinate / SCALE, density cannot drift. Verify
by measuring peak UV-per-world-unit per part: every part should land on 1/SCALE
(0.05). Small shortfalls just mean a part has no face perfectly square to an
axis. Measured spread across the sword library: 0.04921-0.05000.

Side benefit: parts meeting at a join have continuous map2, so a swatch flows
across the seam.

--- MECHANICS -------------------------------------------------------------------
  * map2 must be SECOND in uv_layers. The exporter maps layers in order to
    u0, u1, u2 - second becomes u1, which is CK3's "UV-set 2".
  * Keep map1 as active_render.
  * THE EXPORTER FLIPS V. Blender v -0.283..0.283 becomes file v 0.717..1.283,
    i.e. file_v = 1 - blender_v. U passes through unchanged. Author
    pattern_layout offsets against the FILE values, not Blender's.

--- FOR OTHER WEAPON TYPES ------------------------------------------------------
Same SCALE, always. A dagger, axe or mace authored at a different scale will
tile at a visibly different grain from the swords under one shared layout.


================================================================================
6. AUDIT BEFORE EVERY EXPORT
================================================================================
Must print ALL OK. Anything else ships an invisible weapon.

import bpy, os
SLOTS = [("Base Color","diff"), ("Roughness","spec"), ("Normal","n")]

def resolve(mat, bsdf_input):
    nt = getattr(mat, "node_tree", None)
    if not nt: return None
    out = next((n for n in nt.nodes if n.type=="OUTPUT_MATERIAL"
                and n.inputs["Surface"].is_linked), None)
    if not out: return None
    root = out.inputs["Surface"].links[0].from_node
    if bsdf_input not in root.inputs or not root.inputs[bsdf_input].is_linked:
        return None
    node = root.inputs[bsdf_input].links[0].from_node
    while node.type != "TEX_IMAGE":
        nxt = next((i for i in node.inputs if i.is_linked), None)
        if not nxt: return None
        node = nxt.links[0].from_node
    if node.image is None: return None
    return node.image.filepath_from_user() or None      # "" counts as failure

bad = 0
for obj in sorted(bpy.data.objects, key=lambda o: o.name):
    if obj.type != "MESH" or obj.name.endswith("_ORIGINAL"): continue
    for mat in obj.data.materials:
        if mat is None:
            print("FAIL %s: no material" % obj.name); bad += 1; continue
        got = {s: resolve(mat, i) for i, s in SLOTS}
        missing = [s for s, v in got.items() if not v]
        if missing:
            print("FAIL %s: missing %s" % (obj.name, ", ".join(missing))); bad += 1
        else:
            n = {s: os.path.basename(v) for s, v in got.items()}
            print("ok   %s: %s | %s | %s" % (obj.name, n["diff"], n["n"], n["spec"]))
print("\n" + ("ALL OK" if bad == 0 else "%d PART(S) WILL EXPORT UNTEXTURED" % bad))

Also confirm every referenced image actually exists on disk - a filepath can be
non-empty and still point at nothing.

And assert the UV contract, which the texture audit does not cover:

  assert all([l.name for l in o.data.uv_layers] == ["map1", "map2"] for o in parts)

Wrong order is silent: the exporter maps layers positionally, so a reversed pair
writes the atlas UV into u1 and the projection into u0 - textures and swatch both
land in the wrong space, and nothing errors.


================================================================================
7. EXPORT
================================================================================
Select the 4*N part objects AND the 6*N socket empties. Exclude *_ORIGINAL.

  bpy.ops.io_pdx_mesh.export_mesh(
      filepath=OUT, chk_mesh=True, chk_skel=False,
      chk_locs=True,              # REQUIRED - without it you get no sockets
      chk_selected=True, chk_plain_txt=False)

Note the properties are chk_*, not exp_*.

TWO WAYS THE EXPORT ITSELF FAILS:

  * N-GONS ABORT IT.  "Tangent space can only be computed for tris/quads,
    aborting" - the exporter calls calc_tangents(), which needs tris or quads.
    bmesh.ops.holes_fill() turns a 30-edge ring into ONE n-gon, so ALWAYS
    triangulate after filling:
        bmesh.ops.triangulate(bm, faces=[f for f in bm.faces if len(f.verts)>4])
    Assert it before every export:
        assert not any(len(p.vertices) > 4 for o in parts for p in o.data.polygons)

  * THE ADDON CAN BE DISABLED and the operator simply vanishes:
    'Calling operator "bpy.ops.io_pdx_mesh.export_mesh" error, could not be
    found'. Re-enable in Python rather than hunting through preferences:
        import addon_utils
        addon_utils.enable("bl_ext.user_default.io_pdx_mesh",
                           default_set=True, persistent=True)
Assert before exporting: no "_ORIGINAL" in the selection; counts are 4N and 6N;
every obj.data.name == obj.name + "Shape".

Answers to questions that were once open, all verified by dumping the file:
  * chk_selected=True DOES filter locators by selection.
  * A locator is stored under its OBJECT name.
  * Locator `p` is a world position. We do not parent empties to parts, so no
    parent-space ambiguity arises. Keep it that way.
  * Axis swap: Blender Y (blade axis) becomes file Z. Shapes and locators are
    swapped identically, so if you read both through the same importer they stay
    consistent and need no correction.


================================================================================
8. VERIFY - THE TEST THAT MATTERS
================================================================================
Reassembling a weapon from its own parts, using its sockets, must reproduce the
original weapon's bounds EXACTLY. Drift must be 0.0, not "small".

Do this by reading the exported FILE, not Blender state - it exercises the same
path LoadParts uses. Reader: scratchpad/pdxmesh.py, plus regress.py.

  python regress.py <scratchpad> <path to sword_parts_socketed.mesh>

Expect: shapes=4N, locators=6N, families=N, and "ALL EXACT".

If one family drifts, its socket pair is not at a single point. If ALL drift,
something moved geometry - check contract D.

9. KNOWN QUIRK - SHADER MISMATCH (low priority, recolouring works regardless)
================================================================================
All 14 sword .asset files specify  shader = "portrait_attachment", but only 6 of
14 families carry that in the exported library. The other 8 say "standard",
inherited from the material embedded in their .mesh - the same "the .mesh lies,
the .asset is authoritative" defect as the textures, in the shader field:

  portrait_attachment : african, byzantine, northern, longsword_01_a,
                        longsword_01_b, chinese
  standard            : mena, steppe, western_01_a, western_02_a, western_03_a,
                        western_04_a, prop_western_sword_02, japanese

Deliberately left alone. The per-part shader is not what decides rendering here:
the emitter sets the assembled weapon's shader (it moved to
portrait_attachment_pattern for the pattern system), so this field is inert on
the parts. Both ep1_western_sword_01_a and tgp_japanese_sword_01_a are in the
"standard" group and render correctly in game.

Worth normalising only if the emitter ever starts trusting the per-part value.
It is one line per material - mat["shader"] = "portrait_attachment" - plus a
re-export.

The daggers have the SAME split, and worse - three values instead of two. All 9
dagger .asset files say portrait_attachment, but the exported parts carry:

  court               : ep1_indian, ep1_mena, ep1_western, tgp_japanese
  portrait_attachment : ep2_hunting_knife, fp2_iberian_muslim
  standard            : prop_afr_spymaster, prop_western, tgp_chinese

"court" comes from the court-scene variants of those meshes. Same reasoning
applies: the emitter sets the assembled weapon's shader, so the per-part value
is inert. Do not chase it unless that changes.

9B. TRAPS THAT COST REAL TIME - CHECK THESE
================================================================================
1. OBJECT HIDE vs COLLECTION HIDE are different flags, and select_set() fails
   SILENTLY on an object-level-hidden object. Clear BOTH:

       o.hide_viewport = False      # the monitor icon
       o.hide_set(False)            # the eye icon  <-- easy to forget

   This cost one bad dagger export: ep2_hunting_knife's four parts were
   eye-hidden, never entered the selection, and chk_selected dropped them.
   The file had 32 shapes instead of 36 and nothing errored.

2. ASSERT ON THE SELECTION, NOT ON COLLECTION MEMBERSHIP. The pre-flight above
   passed because it counted objects in collections. Count what is actually
   selected, which is what chk_selected exports:

       sel = [o for o in bpy.context.selected_objects if o.type == 'MESH']
       assert len(sel) == 4 * n_families

3. ALWAYS re-parse the exported file and count shapes. Every silent-drop bug so
   far was caught here and nowhere else.

3B. RUN THE SOCKET-AXIS CHECK. Self-rebuild is NOT sufficient - it passes with
   both sides of a pair wrong by the same amount (see section 4 centring). The
   check that does catch it, and generalises to any future weapon class:

     For every socket pair, measure the LATERAL distance from the socket to the
     AXIAL member's axis at the join. If it is more than ~1.5 units, the socket
     is measuring the wrong thing.

   Two things the checker itself must get right, both learned the hard way:
     * pick the axial member by smallest LATERAL BBOX EXTENT (not longest span);
     * sample the axial member PAST the bevel, using the same >=90%-of-max-extent
       rule that placed the socket. Sampling at the raw join plane produced a
       false FLAG on ep1_western_sword_04_a grip<->guard: the grip's slice there
       is 8 points wide with its centre 1.62 off, but 0.02 further in it is 48
       points and dead on the socket.

   Script: scratchpad/socket_axis_check.py - reads a .mesh, needs no Blender.
   Current state: swords worst 0.088, daggers 0.486, axes 1.113. All clean.

4. Source meshes come with junk to delete before cutting: LOD1/LOD2 (note BOTH
   "_LOD1" and "_LOD_0" spellings exist), PropSetUpScene* locator empties, and
   whole extra objects (weapon racks). The LOD tag is not always a SUFFIX -
   ep2_northern_mace_01_a's objects are named
   "..._portrait_LOD_1_ep2_northern_mace_01_a_po", so match _LOD_?[12](_|$),
   not _LOD_?[12]$.

5. ASSERT THE UV LAYER LIST, NOT JUST ITS PRESENCE:
       assert [l.name for l in o.data.uv_layers] == ["map1","map2"]
   A source with three UV sets otherwise ships a stray u2 (see 5B).

6. FILTER TO type == 'MESH' WHEN SELECTING BY NAME PREFIX. Socket empties share
   the family prefix, so a set comprehension over o.data.materials dies with
   "NoneType has no attribute materials" - empties have no data.

7. THE SOCKET-AXIS CHECKER MUST SAMPLE PAST THE BEVEL, using the same
   >=90%-of-max-extent rule that placed the socket. Sampling at the raw join
   plane produced TWO false positives on spears: ep1_mena_spear_01_a:head read
   1.586 off-axis (the raw slice there is a 1.26 x 0.13 sliver) and
   ep1_western_spear_01_a:cap read "no section" (the plane is exactly the haft's
   end). Re-measured with the correct sampler, all 13 spear sockets are 0.000.

================================================================================
11. JOIN RADIUS - WHICH PARTS CAN ACTUALLY MIX
================================================================================
Sockets align parts correctly; they cannot make a thin rod fill a wide bore.
A part whose join radius is far from the library norm will LOOK wrong even
though every check passes.

MEASURE, per family: the anchor's cross-section extent at its own socket.

  library   anchor  median join dia   outlier
  --------  ------  ----------------  ------------------------------------
  maces     haft    ~3.37             ep1_indian_mace_01_a  8.51  (2.5x)
  axes      haft    ~3.15             none beyond ~1.7x
  swords    grip    ~2.7              none significant
  daggers   grip    ~2.2              none significant

RULE OF THUMB: if a new anchor measures much over TWICE the library median at
its own socket, every foreign lead bored for a normal shaft will float on it.
Say so when you add it. ep1_indian_mace_01_a is restricted to self-only in code
for exactly this reason - its haft is a cone that widens toward the head.

A SECOND, INDEPENDENT AXIS OF INCOMPATIBILITY - mounting style. Measure the
lead's own mount depth (how far the anchor penetrates it):

  * ep1_indian_mace_01_a's head has NO cross-section at its own join plane -
    a flat BUTT joint, zero interpenetration, where every other mace head has
    a bore the haft seats into. Two independent reasons to keep it self-only.
  * fp2_iberian_muslim_dagger_01_a's guard genuinely swallows 7.61 units of
    blade against <=0.46 for every other dagger. It survives because the join
    is rod-anchored (section 4), but it is the first place a scheme change
    would show.

Distinguish GENUINE deep mounting from a bbox artifact by testing ENCLOSURE at
the join plane: does the fitting's cross-section CONTAIN the rod's? If they sit
side by side instead, the overlap is a part running ALONGSIDE (a knuckle-bow)
and the cap in section 4 already handles it correctly.
  ep1_mena_sword_01_a grip<->guard raw 9.32 = ALONGSIDE, spurious.
  fp2_iberian_muslim_dagger guard<->blade raw 7.61 = GENUINE.

After exporting, the generator run prints one line per library. A new family is

  no <anchor> found for weapon family <name>   missing the held part  (HARD)
  no sockets on <part>                         locators missing/misnamed (HARD)

================================================================================
13. ADDITIVE REPAIR - WHEN A PART IS VISUALLY BROKEN
================================================================================
Done once: ep1_western_hammer_01_a_head.

SYMPTOM. The head read as two disconnected lumps - a pick and a hammer face
with a gap between them. The gap was not empty: it was filled by the HAFT. The
head's only geometry there was 32 verts sitting at EXACTLY the haft's footprint
(x +-1.575, z -2.266..1.935) - thin caps coplanar with the haft surface, so they
z-fought and contributed nothing. Invisible while both parts share a texture;
obvious once the pattern system recolours head and haft differently.

FIX. A 6-quad collar enclosing the haft through the gap band:
      collar  x +-1.72   y -41.62..-38.64   z -2.78..2.98
      haft    x +-1.575                     z -2.585..2.76
Overlap into BOTH neighbours (pick z>=2.44, face z<=-2.27) so it merges rather
than butts. A first attempt at z -2.45..2.60 left thin haft slivers showing at
the silhouette edges - size the collar to enclose the anchor's FULL extent.

THE TWO CONDITIONS THAT KEEP IT LEGAL (contract D):
  * no existing vertex moves;
  * the part's bbox is unchanged - verify before and after.
Both held here (122 -> 128 faces, bbox identical), so the socket, which derives
from the haft axis, did not move and self-rebuild stayed exact.

Also: quads only, map1 UVs copied from the nearest existing vertex of the SAME
part so the new faces sample that part's own texture, map2 recomputed from the
world projection at 20.0. Reuse the existing PDX material - a fresh one has no
"shader" property and the part silently vanishes from the export.

14. SPEARS AND POLEARMS
================================================================================
8 families, hafted taxonomy (head / haft / cap), haft is the anchor.

  3-slot: ep1_african_spear_01_a   ep1_indian_spear_01_a
          ep1_western_spear_01_a   ep2_lance_01_a
          tgp_chinese_polearm_02
  2-slot: ep1_mena_spear_01_a      ep2_boar_spear_01_a
          ep2_boar_spear_01_b

WHAT IS DIFFERENT ABOUT SPEARS
  * They are LONG - 192 to 266 units against 78-130 for swords - and the shaft
    is almost free of geometry. Heads routinely hold 70-93% of the faces
    (ep1_mena_spear_01_a: 708 of 764). A head-heavy split is normal here, not a
    misclassification. Verify by eye, not by face ratio.
  * The dense part of a spear "head" is the COLLAR / binding just below the
    blade, not the blade. Classify the collar with the head - it is the socket
    the shaft seats into, and cutting between blade and collar leaves a part
    that cannot mount to anything.
  * The axe "long round island -> haft" override MUST BE OFF. See section 3, 1B.

REJECTED, AND WHY IT GENERALISES
  tgp_chinese_polearm_01 - the court/display model. Bundles
    ep1_western_weaponrack_01 AND is authored along Z. Superseded by the _02
    portrait variant, which is bare and already on +Y.
  ep2_lance_01_b - byte-identical geometry to _01_a (same vertex hash), differing
    only in texture. Same situation as ep2_northern_mace_01_b. With recolouring
    now procedural, a texture-only twin adds no silhouette variety. Trivial to
    reinstate if the texture variant is ever wanted.

  Compare ep2_boar_spear_01_a vs _01_b: same poly AND vertex counts, DIFFERENT
  hashes - genuinely different geometry, both kept. Always hash before assuming
  an a/b pair is a duplicate, and always hash before assuming it is not.

MIXING CAVEAT - ep2_lance_01_a IS AN OUTLIER AT BOTH ENDS
  Its head is a 248-face tip that reads as a bare point on another shaft, and its
  "cap" is a 628-face vamplate + grip + butt assembly, not a small ferrule. Both
  are honest mappings of unusual geometry. Expect them to look extreme in a mix;
  neither is a defect.

TEXTURES
  tgp_chinese_polearm_02's maps live in the _01 model's folder
  (artifacts/ep4/tgp_chinese_polearm/), with the diffuse from _02 but the normal
  and properties from _01. Another instance of section 5: go to the .asset, and
  never assume the textures sit beside the mesh or share its name.
